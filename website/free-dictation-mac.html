Lipik and typical cloud dictation apps

Many dictation apps send your recording to a server, turn it into text there, and charge a monthly fee or limit how many words you can use for free. Lipik does the work on your Mac instead. Details differ from app to app, so check each app's own site.

What runs on your Mac

Speech to text uses Parakeet by default, an English model. Lipik downloads it once from Hugging Face; it is 731 MB. For other languages, you can download one of the free Whisper models in Settings. Together they cover 99 languages, and Hinglish works in dictation (beta) and in meeting notes.

The clean-up step removes filler words, fixes punctuation and writes in the tone you set for each kind of app. It is optional, and it is built in, so no other app is needed. One button in setup downloads the clean-up model from Hugging Face: Qwen3 8B (5 GB) on Macs with 16 GB of memory or more, or Qwen3 4B Instruct (2.5 GB) on smaller Macs. It runs on your Mac. Built-in clean-up needs macOS 13.3 or later. On older versions, you can use the free Ollama app instead. Without a clean-up model, Lipik still types what you said, and its built-in rules still fix "um", spoken punctuation, numbers, times and emails.

If you'd rather use a cloud model for clean-up, you can add your own API key. Only then is text sent to that provider, and only the text, not your recording.

What needs the internet

Lipik goes online only for these:

  • one-time downloads from Hugging Face: the speech model (731 MB), the clean-up model (2.5 or 5 GB) and, for meeting notes, the speaker model (33 MB) and, the first time a meeting is in Hinglish, the Hinglish speech model (about 2 GB)
  • Apple's English (India) speech files, through macOS, for Hinglish dictation only
  • every few hours, a check with GitHub for the latest version number, to show a "new version" notice. It carries nothing about you, and you can turn it off in Settings, under Privacy, then Updates.
  • every few hours, an anonymous count so we know how many people use Lipik: a random ID, the app version, the macOS version and the chip type. Never your voice, text or name. Turn it off in Settings, under Privacy, then Usage count.
  • your email, only if you type it during setup to hear about new versions
  • cloud clean-up, only if you add your own API key

After the downloads, dictation works with Wi-Fi off. There is no account and there are no ads, and nothing you say leaves your Mac: your voice and your text stay on it. On its own, Lipik sends only the update check and the anonymous usage count above, and you can turn both off in Settings, under Privacy.

Questions

Is Lipik really free?

Yes. Every feature is free, with no subscription, no account and no word limit. The code is open source under the MIT licence.

Does Lipik work offline?

Yes, once the models are downloaded. The speech model is a one-time 731 MB download from Hugging Face, and the optional clean-up model is a one-time 2.5 or 5 GB download from the same place. After that, Lipik works with Wi-Fi off.

Does Lipik send my voice anywhere?

No. Your voice is turned into text on your Mac, and the recording stays there. There is no account, and your voice and text never leave your Mac. If you add your own API key for cloud clean-up, the text, not the recording, goes to the provider you chose.

Which Macs does it run on?

Macs with Apple silicon (M1 or later). There is no Windows version yet.

Why does macOS say Lipik can't be opened?

Lipik isn't signed with an Apple Developer ID yet, so macOS blocks a downloaded copy the first time. The easiest way around it is to install from Terminal: paste curl -fsSL https://lipik.pages.dev/install.sh | sh and press Return. With the download, on macOS 15 and later, double-click Lipik, click Done, then go to System Settings, then Privacy & Security, and click Open Anyway.
